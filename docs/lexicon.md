# Lexicon and style guide: the voice of the Well

*The Well of Souls / Le Puits des Âmes* is dark fantasy: a bottomless well under a sleeping
village, stolen souls, wardens in the dark, a shrine's lantern and its bell. This guide sets how
the game speaks, in French first and in English. Read it before you name anything or write a
line of lore. For the routine of adding and translating texts, and the rules glossary, see
`docs/translating.md`.

## (a) Principles of the register

1. **Weight before wit.** Names are heavy, old and final, never cute or jokey. A boss is not a
   "Giant Frog" but *La Matrone du Bourbier*. A death is not "You died" but *Le Puits te réclame*.
2. **Concrete, old nouns.** Stone, ash, bone, bell, wax, rust, salt, marrow, shroud, ember, mire,
   ossuary, pyre. Use the noun a medieval chronicler or a priest would use, not a lab or an
   office word.
3. **Few words, strong verbs.** Two or three words for a name, one verb for a button. *Sonner le
   glas*, *S'agenouiller*, *Prêter serment*, *Embraser*, *Terrasser*. (Dark Souls III FR: *Embraser*
   = light a bonfire; *Étreindre la Flamme*; *Entretenir la Première Flamme*.)
4. **Liturgy and ruin.** The Well is worshipped and feared. Use rites (vigil, communion, litany,
   penance, relic, offering, oath), holy objects gone wrong (a sun that is dead, a saint's heart
   in a jar) and the language of loss (fallen, forsaken, hollow, drowned, nameless).
5. **The dark is patient.** Lore lines are short, solemn and in the present tense. They imply more
   than they say. They end on an image, not an explanation. No exclamation marks in lore.
6. **Rules stay plain.** Dark fantasy dresses **names, titles, lore, flavor, banners and the
   buttons that start things**. It never touches numbers, stats or rules (see (e)).
7. **Kagari is Japanese-rooted.** The village, the shrine, the ninja keeper and some yokai
   (Kappa, Tengu, the paper lantern) stay. Their tone is grave too: a *rōnin* (FR *rônin*), a
   bell, a shrine, not a cartoon.

## (c) Naming patterns

| Pattern | Seen in | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| **Name + epithet** (le + participle or noun) | *Margit le Déchu*, *Mohg le Réprouvé*, *Lurien le Veilleur*, *Yhorm le Géant* | *Gorr le Borgne* / *Gorr the One-Eyed* |
| **Name, apposition** | *Aldrich, Dévoreur de dieux*; *Ebrietas, fille du Cosmos* | boss cards: name, then its title (*Gardien de l'Ossuaire*) |
| **The + noun + "de" a place or an abstraction** | *Vordt de la Vallée boréale*, *Dame à la Robe traînante*, *Crisanta du Linceul d'agonie* | *La Matrone du Bourbier*, *Gardienne des Eaux Croupies* |
| **Role title** (one noun) | *La Pleureuse*, *Le Pénitent* (Blasphemous), *Le Croisé* (Darkest Dungeon) | *La Pleureuse* / *The Mourner* |
| **Hyphenated compound** | *Porte-Braises*, *Arbre-Monde*, *Sans-éclat*, *Vertchemin* | *Le Temple-Bûcher* / *The Pyre Temple* |
| **Participle state** | *Exsangue*, *Desséché*, *Écorché*, *Ramené par les Flammes* | ranks *Funeste*, *Céleste*; curse *Étreinte du Givre* |
| **"Celui qui..."** (a riddle as a name) | *Celui qui renaquit* (Bloodborne) | for future secret bosses |
| **Rite or object as a place** | *Sépultures oubliées*, *Le Chœur*, *Calice de Pthumeru* | *L'Ossuaire de Kagari*, *Les Archives Muettes* |
| **Item: "de" + owner** | *Anneau du Gardien*, *Larme imitatrice* | *Cœur de Saint*, *Carquois de l'Hydre* |
| **Achievement: a phrase with an echo** | *Ne craignez pas le faucheur*, *De la poussière à la poussière*, *Accepter le vide* | *Arpenteur de l'Abîme*, *Nourrir l'Abîme*, *Lueur dans le Noir* |

House rules:

- **Length.** Names 2 to 3 words (a boss 4 at most, with its article), FR under about 24 characters
  for a card or a button, 16 for a HUD banner. French runs 20 to 30% longer: write the French
  first, then check it on screen.
- **Capitals.** House style is Title Case for names in both languages (*Grotte Suintante*), on
  pixel-font cards that read better. FromSoftware's French often lowercases trailing adjectives
  (*Vallée boréale*). Either is correct; we keep ours consistent.
- **Articles.** Unique beings and places take the article (*La Pleureuse*, *L'Ossuaire de Kagari*);
  kinds of monsters and items don't (*Squelette*, *Hache de Guerre*).
- **Base gear stays plain.** As in Dark Souls (*Épée longue*, *Hache de bataille*), a weapon or armor
  type keeps its smith's name (*Katana*, *Cotte de Mailles*). The dark voice goes into the
  affixes (*Cerclé de Fer*, *des Ronces*), the uniques, the relics and the item lines.
- **Agreement.** French adjectives agree with the noun (`src/core/grammar.gd`). A new item or elite
  affix gets its `grammar:feminine:` row; a new monster or item its `grammar:gender:` row. Rank
  names in monster names are not agreed: pick invariable words (*Funeste*, *Céleste*,
  *Légendaire*).
- **Characters.** The pixel font has the French accents, œ Œ « » and – —, but avoid dashes and
  rare signs inside names, and macrons (*rōnin*: write *rônin* / *Ronin*).

## (d) Words to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| super, méga, ultra, génial, cool, top | modern, chatty | (nothing) or *grand*, *funeste* |
| bonus, upgrade, power-up, boost, buff, combo (in **names** or lore) | arcade and app words | *don*, *faveur*, *bénédiction*, *atout* (rules) |
| loot (in lore), drop | gamer slang | *butin* in rules, *dépouilles*, *trésor* in names |
| Chromatique, shiny | a Pokémon word | *Céleste* / *Celestial* |
| Épique (for a monster), XP, level up (in lore) | game-system words in fiction | *Funeste* / *Fell*; *s'endurcir* in lore |
| OK, ça brille !, cadeau | flat, childish | a real line, or keep the rule plain |
| truck, camion, laser, robot, ninja-cool | anachronisms | *bélier*, *rayon*, *automate* |
| "Appel", "Répondre à l'appel" | generic, reads like a phone | *Glas*, *Sonner le glas* |
| exclamation marks in lore | shouting | a full stop; let the image work |
| Title Case in sentences, English word order in French | translation smell | rewrite the sentence in French |

## (e) Rules stay plain

Dark fantasy is for **names, titles, lore, flavor lines, banners and the buttons that start
things**. It is **not** for numbers and rules. A rule line keeps its numbers exact and uses the
stat words of the glossary in `docs/translating.md`, one word per idea:

| English | Français |
|---|---|
| Health / max Health | Vie / Vie max |
| Damage | Dégâts |
| Armor | Armure |
| Move speed / Attack speed | Vitesse / Vitesse d'attaque |
| Critical hit (chance) | coup critique (chance de coup critique) |
| Souls / Gold | Âmes / Or |
| Perk / Relic / Family / Rune | Atout / Relique / Famille / Rune |
| Floor / Act / Stairs / Seal | Étage / Acte / Escaliers / Sceau |
| Elite (the kind of monster) | élite |
| Item rarities Common / Magic / Rare / Epic | Commun / Magique / Rare / Épique |

So: *"Sous la moitié de ta Vie, tu infliges 20 % de Dégâts en plus par niveau."* stays as it is,
while the perk's **name** can be *Rage Sanguinaire*. A curse has a dark name and lore
(*Étreinte du Givre*: "Le Puits expire...") and a plain rule (*Réchauffe-toi près des feux de
camp, ou gèle.*). Buttons that only manage menus (*Retour*, *Fermer*, *Acheter*, *Vendre*,
*Options*) stay plain too: only the ones that **start** something get a voice (*Descendre*,
*Sonner le glas*, *S'agenouiller*).

## (f) Voice: who speaks

- **The narrator** (story pages, act cards, curse lore, game over): grave, present tense, short
  sentences, one image each. Speaks to the player as **tu** in French, "you" in English. Never
  jokes. *"Le Puits te réclame. La cloche, loin là-haut, sonne pour toi."*
- **The Well** (curse lore, floor events, banners): an old hunger, not a character with lines. It
  *breathes, stirs, swallows, claims*. It never says "I".
- **The keepers** (the merchant, the smith, the spirits at the encounters): the only ones who
  talk in quotes. Weary, dry, a little wry: the dark humour of people who stayed too long down
  there (*"Les morts n'achètent pas grand-chose."*). The Old Wanderer is a scavenger, Brann the
  smith is curt, the Grinning Spirit is a gambler.
- **The shrine** (Tree of Souls, oaths): solemn and ritual. Oaths are sworn, souls are offered,
  the bell tolls at dawn.
- **French:** the player is always **tu** (*Descends*, *Ta lanterne s'éteint*). Typography: a space
  before `: ; ! ?` and `%`, quotes as `« ... »`.
